Hello, I'm Dr. Takuma Sato, a fertility specialist.

It's common for individuals on their fertility journey (TTC) to wonder about which nutrients might support their efforts. Among many nutrients, zinc has been highlighted for its potential deep involvement in both male and female reproductive functions, with research focusing on sperm health and oocyte quality.

This article will provide evidence-based information on the potential roles of zinc in fertility and its consumption.

## Zinc: Its Importance in Male Reproductive Function

Zinc is a critically important mineral for male reproductive function. It's involved in numerous enzymatic reactions, supporting essential life processes like DNA synthesis, cell division, and protein synthesis.

### Impact on Sperm Formation and Function

That zinc is a nutrient involved in sperm formation is well established biochemically. The question is whether **supplementing with it changes the outcome.**

There is good-quality data here. A 2025 meta-analysis pooling 50 randomised trials of 12 weeks or longer found **no improvement in pregnancy or live birth rates from supplements generally** (PMID: 40431450). Broken down by semen parameter, **zinc combined with folic acid was reported to improve sperm concentration** — but the studies were rated low quality.

Beyond that, a large randomised trial gave **30 mg of zinc and 5 mg of folic acid for six months to 2,370 men**. Live birth was 34% in the supplement group and 35% on placebo — no difference — and sperm concentration, motility, morphology and volume were all unchanged. **Sperm DNA fragmentation was in fact slightly higher** (29.7% vs 27.2%). Abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting were also more common than in the control group (PMID: 31910279).

In short: individual semen parameters may shift a little, but **the chance of having a child did not change.**

This is a useful case for a general point: a nutrient being necessary for a process does not mean that adding more of it improves the outcome. Correcting a deficiency and topping up someone who is already sufficient are different things.

## Zinc: Potential Contribution to Female Reproductive Function and Oocyte Quality

Compared to male fertility, robust human clinical evidence regarding zinc's role in female reproductive function is still developing. However, basic research and small-scale zinc oocyte quality RCTs suggest several possibilities.

### Oocyte Maturation and Quality

Zinc is indicated to play an indispensable role in the processes of oocyte growth and maturation, particularly in the normal progression of meiosis. It's also believed that its antioxidant properties might mitigate damage to oocytes from oxidative stress, potentially contributing to the maintenance of oocyte quality. This has been explored in studies evaluating the impact on oocyte quality and fertilization rates in women undergoing in vitro fertilization.

### Limitations of Information: Current Evidence Status

Currently, there's a scarcity of definitive large-scale randomized controlled trials (RCTs) demonstrating that zinc supplementation in women directly improves pregnancy rates or live birth rates. While the evidence for male infertility is relatively stronger, the effects in women remain at a stage where further research is awaited.

## Zinc Intake in Daily Life

Zinc is abundant in foods such as oysters, beef, pork liver, eel, nuts, and legumes. Actively incorporating these foods through a balanced diet can contribute to general health maintenance.

However, it's crucial to avoid simplistic assumptions like "consuming zinc guarantees pregnancy." Excessive intake of specific nutrients can potentially have adverse effects on the body. If you are considering using supplements, always consult with a doctor or pharmacist to receive appropriate advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Is zinc truly effective for male infertility?
A1: Zinc is genuinely a nutrient involved in sperm formation. However, **supplementing with it has not been shown to raise pregnancy or live birth rates.** In a trial of 2,370 men, live birth was 34% versus 35%, and DNA fragmentation rose slightly (PMID: 31910279). If your diet already supplies enough, there is currently no basis for adding more.

### Q2: Should women also take zinc?
A2: Zinc is an essential mineral for various physiological functions in women as well, and it's suggested that it may contribute to oocyte growth and quality maintenance. However, definitive evidence that zinc supplementation in women directly improves pregnancy or live birth rates is currently lacking. Base your intake on a balanced diet, and consult with a doctor regarding supplements.

### Q3: Is excessive zinc intake dangerous?
A3: Yes, excessive zinc intake can lead to side effects such as anemia due to copper absorption inhibition, decreased immune function, nausea, and diarrhea. While overconsumption is rare from a normal diet, it's important to adhere to recommended intake levels when using supplements. Always follow the instructions of a doctor or pharmacist.

## Conclusion

Zinc matters biochemically for sperm formation and for oocyte maturation. What the trials show is that supplementing it does not raise the chance of a pregnancy or a birth — in the largest trial, not even the semen parameters moved. For women, there is no large trial showing an effect at all. Getting enough zinc from food is sensible; buying it as a fertility supplement is not supported.

Consuming zinc through a balanced diet is recommended for general health maintenance. If you are considering using supplements, please consult with a specialist physician to receive appropriate advice tailored to your specific condition.

## The Fundamental Role of Folate and the Spotlight on "Activated" Forms

Folate, a B vitamin, is essential for cell division and DNA synthesis. During early pregnancy, it plays a vital role in the healthy development of the fetus, particularly the formation of the brain and spinal cord. This is why health authorities worldwide, including the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, recommend that women planning pregnancy supplement with 400 µg of folic acid daily, in addition to dietary intake.

The folate we consume broadly falls into two categories: synthetic folic acid (pteroylmonoglutamic acid) found in supplements and some fortified foods, and natural polyglutamate folate found in food. Both forms must be converted into their active form, methylfolate, in the body before they can function.

This is where the "MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) gene mutation" becomes relevant. Individuals with this mutation may have a reduced ability to convert folic acid into its active methylfolate form. Consequently, a debate has emerged: wouldn't it be more efficient for those with MTHFR gene mutations to take methylfolate, which is already in its active form?

## MTHFR Gene Mutation and the Evidence for Methylfolate

MTHFR gene mutations are known to cause individual differences in folate metabolism. For individuals with this mutation, taking methylfolate can potentially supply folate in a form directly usable by the body, which some studies suggest may more efficiently maintain folate levels compared to standard folic acid.

However, for the general healthy adult female population without MTHFR gene mutations, **many studies currently show no significant difference in efficacy between standard folic acid and methylfolate.** Furthermore, there is no definitive conclusion that methylfolate is "dramatically superior" to standard folic acid for pregnancy rates, outcomes, or birth results, irrespective of MTHFR mutations. The potential benefits of methylfolate tend to be more pronounced in individuals carrying MTHFR gene mutations.

It's important to remember that the primary goal of folate supplementation is to reduce the risk of neural tube defects, and standard folic acid has proven effective for this purpose. If you have concerns about your MTHFR gene status, please consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice. Our [homepage](/en/ 'homepage') provides various resources on preconception care.

## Understanding the Term "Activated"

While the term "activated" can sound very appealing and suggest superiority over regular folic acid, its effectiveness and necessity must be carefully evaluated based on scientific evidence in a medical context. While methylfolate may offer potential benefits for some individuals, it is not a "magic bullet" for everyone.

The choice of supplement depends on an individual's health status, diet, genetic background, and most importantly, their specific goals. ## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Do I really need methylfolate if I have an MTHFR gene mutation?

A1: Not necessarily. Even with an MTHFR gene mutation, standard folic acid can still be effective in reducing the risk of neural tube defects for many individuals. However, depending on the type of mutation and your individual circumstances, methylfolate might be utilized more efficiently by your body. If you are concerned, please consult a fertility specialist. It's crucial to understand your own situation to make the best choice.

### Q2: Are there any special benefits of taking methylfolate supplements?

A2: As methylfolate is already in its active form, it may offer more efficient folate supplementation for individuals with MTHFR gene mutations who have reduced folate metabolism capabilities. However, for the general population, robust evidence definitively showing that methylfolate is superior to standard folic acid in terms of pregnancy rates or outcomes is not yet established. It's important to have a balanced understanding of the information and avoid unrealistic expectations.

### Q3: How should I choose a folic acid supplement for my TTC journey?

A3: First, ensure the supplement contains the recommended daily amount of 400 µg of folic acid (pteroylmonoglutamic acid). The choice between methylfolate and standard folic acid can be made based on your individual constitution and your doctor's recommendation. Also, prioritize supplements from reputable manufacturers with reliable quality and production standards. Regardless of the type of folate you choose, consulting with a healthcare provider and selecting a supplement that aligns with your body and goals is most important.

## Conclusion

Methylfolate may be a valuable option, particularly for individuals with MTHFR gene mutations, to support folate metabolism. However, for the general population, standard folic acid remains effective in reducing the risk of neural tube defects. The key is not to be swayed by claims that a specific supplement is "absolutely superior," but rather to understand your body's condition and goals, and make choices based on reliable medical information.

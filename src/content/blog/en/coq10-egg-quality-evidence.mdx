For anyone trying to conceive, egg quality is one of the topics that weighs most heavily on the mind. And you have probably come across information recommending CoQ10 (coenzyme Q10) as a way to improve it. In in vitro fertilization in particular, it has been suggested that CoQ10 may contribute to better egg quality and pregnancy rates in the group described as poor responders, whose ovaries respond weakly to stimulation, and its usefulness in that group has been examined in studies in Japan and abroad.

But what does taking CoQ10 mean for the far larger number of women trying to conceive who are not poor responders? Questions such as "is it effective for everyone?" and "how far has the evidence actually been established?" are entirely reasonable. If you feel a vague unease, that feeling is not wrong. In this article I want to dig into current scientific knowledge on CoQ10 and egg quality from a calm and objective point of view. I hope it helps you make decisions on your own fertility journey.

## What Is Coenzyme Q10?

Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) is a coenzyme produced naturally in the body and present in almost all of our cells. It plays an essential role in the function of the mitochondria, which are responsible for producing energy inside the cell. It also has strong antioxidant properties, and by protecting cells from oxidative stress it is thought to contribute to maintaining cellular function.

Although the body makes CoQ10 for itself, the amount produced tends to fall with age. It can also be obtained from food, but taking in a sufficient amount that way is considered difficult.

## Egg Quality and the Role of Mitochondria

Egg quality is an important factor deeply connected to fertilization, embryo development, implantation and the continuation of pregnancy. Many things influence egg quality, but among them mitochondrial function is thought to be very important. The egg contains an especially large number of mitochondria compared with other cells in the body, because fertilization and early embryo development require a great deal of energy.

When mitochondrial function declines, the egg's capacity to produce energy falls, and it has been suggested that egg quality may deteriorate as a result. Oxidative stress is also said to damage mitochondria and accelerate the aging of the egg. For these reasons CoQ10, which supports mitochondrial function and has antioxidant activity, has attracted attention as a supplement that may improve egg quality.

## The Evidence for CoQ10 in Poor Responders

In in vitro fertilization, several studies have suggested that CoQ10 supplementation may improve egg quality and clinical outcomes in patients described as poor responders, whose ovaries respond poorly to stimulation. In these studies, taking CoQ10 for a period before the start of ovarian stimulation has been reported as possibly increasing the number of eggs retrieved, improving embryo quality and improving pregnancy rates. The reasoning rests on the hypothesis that CoQ10 supports mitochondrial function and improves the microenvironment within the ovary, thereby favorably influencing follicular development and egg maturation.

## What About Women Who Are Not Poor Responders?

So what can be expected of CoQ10 for people undergoing more standard fertility treatment, or aiming for natural conception, who are not poor responders?

Recent randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses in humans have reported a variety of results regarding the effect of CoQ10 on egg quality and pregnancy outcomes.

*   **Positive reports**: some studies have suggested that, particularly in older women and in women with unexplained infertility, CoQ10 supplementation may contribute to better egg maturation rates, higher blastocyst formation rates and even improved clinical pregnancy rates. This is thought to reflect the possibility that the mitochondrial and antioxidant effects of CoQ10 also benefit the eggs of these groups.
*   **Limited or unclear reports**: on the other hand, there are study results indicating that CoQ10 cannot be said to be consistently effective across all patient groups. In large RCTs in particular, there are reports that CoQ10 supplementation produced no statistically significant improvement in egg quality or pregnancy rates. Because study designs, CoQ10 doses, durations of use and the patient groups studied are all diverse, the current picture is one of variable results.

At present, **strong evidence has not yet been established** that would justify recommending CoQ10 across the board to every woman trying to conceive who is not a poor responder. The possibility has been suggested, but the extent of any benefit and the best way to take it await further large-scale research. For your own situation, a conversation with a specialist is essential.

Taking CoQ10 may be something to consider as part of a healthy life overall. For general information about preconception care, [this article](/blog/preconception-care-today) may also be helpful.

## Points to Keep in Mind If You Are Considering CoQ10

If you are thinking about taking CoQ10, the following points matter.

1.  **The importance of talking with your doctor**: supplements are not medicines, but there are many things to consider, including your own constitution and health status and interactions with other medications you take. Always consult your primary doctor or your fertility specialist and confirm whether it is appropriate for you.
2.  **Product quality**: CoQ10 supplements vary enormously. It is important to choose a product with attention to quality, content and absorbability.
3.  **A comprehensive approach**: a supplement such as CoQ10 is only one element supporting your efforts to conceive. Improving the basics of daily life, a balanced diet, moderate exercise, sufficient sleep and stress management, matters more than anything for maintaining overall health, including egg quality. ## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Should everyone take CoQ10?

**A1**: CoQ10 is a substance the body produces, and because production tends to decline with age, some people consider taking it for general health. When the aim is specifically to improve egg quality while trying to conceive, usefulness has been suggested for the particular group of patients called poor responders, but clear evidence to recommend it uniformly to everyone has not been established at this point. Rather than starting on your own judgment without consulting a doctor, please talk with a specialist about your own situation.

### Q2: Will taking CoQ10 definitely improve my egg quality?

**A2**: Unfortunately there is no evidence strong enough to state that egg quality will "definitely" improve. It has been suggested that CoQ10 may support mitochondrial function and antioxidant activity, both relevant to egg quality, but effects vary between individuals and study results are not consistent. Egg quality is influenced not only by CoQ10 but by many factors, including age, genetics and lifestyle.

### Q3: Are there ways other than CoQ10 to support egg quality?

**A3**: There is no miracle cure that directly improves egg quality, but generally healthy habits are thought to support the health of the egg. Specifically, these include a balanced diet, moderate exercise, sufficient sleep, not smoking, limiting alcohol and managing stress appropriately. Taking certain nutrients, folic acid among them, is also recommended. For details, please consult your own doctor or a registered dietitian.

## Summary

By supporting mitochondrial function and antioxidant activity in the egg, CoQ10 has been suggested as possibly contributing to better egg quality and pregnancy rates, particularly in poor responder patients. For women trying to conceive more generally, however, strong evidence that CoQ10 reliably improves egg quality is still not sufficient.

What is best for you depends on your individual circumstances and health. Feeling anxious in the face of uncertain information is natural. If you are considering CoQ10, please talk it through with a specialist and judge calmly where it fits within your overall plan.
